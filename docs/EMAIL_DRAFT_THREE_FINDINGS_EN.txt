Subject: SINDBAD–WISP update: 293 regions analysed and three provisional findings

Hi Nuno, hi Sujan,

I have analysed a snapshot of 293 regions from WISP_V1_202610, comparing No_Fire and VIIRS_Fire. Of these, 247 pass the primary physical QA gate; the remaining 46 are flagged and kept separate from the main analysis. The attached three figures show selected cases from the QA-passed subset.

1. Productivity alone can miss a carbon-stock response (Sétif, Algeria).
The VIIRS-fire simulation has only 0.99% lower mean annual GPP over 2015–2023, but 13.4% less vegetation carbon at the end of 2023. This suggests that biomass stocks may provide a stronger disturbance constraint than GPP alone. The next question is which pools and allocation/turnover processes explain the difference; this is not yet evidence of a particular recovery mechanism.

2. Direct combustion changes the annual sink/source diagnosis (near Vila Real, Portugal).
In 2016, VIIRS-fire NEE is −25.3 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹, implying net uptake if considered alone. Adding 33.4 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ of direct fire carbon gives +8.2 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹: a net source in this modelled case. I independently recomputed these totals from the original daily outputs. This is particularly relevant to T9.2: NEE and direct combustion need to be reported separately and combined consistently.

3. A joint carbon–water mismatch deserves priority diagnosis (two Nile Delta regions).
Both scenarios produce much lower GPP and ET than both MODIS and FLUXCOM-X on matched reference cells and years. The discrepancy persists without fire, so we should first examine water supply, land-cover/support and parameterization rather than attribute it to fire. The references differ substantially in magnitude, but agree on the direction of this discrepancy.

These are exploratory model results, not observed fire-emission estimates or Mediterranean-wide conclusions. The current forcing uses the VIIRS-based fire proxy; genuine WISP predictions are not included in this comparison.

Do these three directions look useful for the next stage: stock-versus-flux constraints, complete fire carbon accounting, and targeted diagnosis of joint GPP/ET biases?
